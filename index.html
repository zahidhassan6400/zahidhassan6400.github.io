Research profile

Process-aware device research with experimental depth.

My work asks a practical question: how can thin-film composition, plasma conditions, layer architecture, and interfaces be engineered into stable, reproducible semiconductor devices?

I combine cleanroom process integration with electrical and materials characterization to move from a fabrication problem to a device-physics explanation. This end-to-end approach supports both research discovery and semiconductor process development.

Selected research directions

Engineering the material–process–device connection.

Focused investigations that connect sputtering plasma, thin-film structure, defect behavior, and transistor reliability.

01

Channel architecture

ITO/ZnO nanolaminate TFTs

Engineering ultrathin layer sequence, thickness, and asymmetry to balance carrier transport, threshold control, reproducibility, and operational stability.

  • Nanolaminates
  • Interface engineering
  • Charge transport
02

Plasma-process engineering

O₂/Ar control of sputtered ITO

Investigating how the O₂/Ar ratio in the sputtering plasma changes ITO crystallization, carrier transport, electrical resistance, and wet-etch response.

  • Gas-ratio control
  • Crystallization
  • Etch behavior
03

Defect physics

Electron trapping in ZnO

Resolving how O₂ plasma treatment and thermal annealing regulate defect states, electron trapping/detrapping, and bias-dependent device stability.

  • O₂ plasma
  • Annealing
  • Trap dynamics
04

Device reliability

Electrical, thermal, and photo-stability

Quantifying threshold-voltage evolution under positive and negative bias stress, temperature, and illumination across reproducible TFT populations.

  • PBS/NBS
  • Temperature
  • Illumination
